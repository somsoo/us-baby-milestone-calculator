🧮 How this calculator works

The calculator counts the days from your baby's due date to today on your device's calendar. Each leap window uses the weeks-after-due-date ranges in the leap chart published by The Wonder Weeks, the book and app by Frans Plooij and Hetty van de Rijt. A day is inside a leap when it falls on or after the start week and before the end week, so leap 4, for example, runs from day 98 to day 139 after the due date.

  • Day 0 is the due date itself, and weeks are counted from that day.
  • Dates use your local calendar, so time zones and daylight saving time do not shift them.
  • The windows show when fussy signs are most likely. Every baby is different, and some show very few signs.

Source: leap week ranges from the official leap chart at thewonderweeks.com, checked September 30, 2026. The descriptions on this page are our own summaries.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Why use the due date instead of the birth date?

Leap timing follows how far development has come since conception, so it is counted from the due date. A baby born two weeks early usually reaches each leap about two weeks later by birthday, and a baby born late reaches it earlier.

How long does a leap last?

It varies. The fussy phase can last from a day to several weeks, and the windows in the table show when signs are most likely, not a fixed schedule. Some babies show very few signs.

My baby was born early. What do I enter?

Enter the original due date. If you only know the birth date, open 'Only know the birth date?' and enter the birth date with the weeks and days of pregnancy at birth. The calculator works out the due date for you.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is general information about a popular leap model and cannot tell whether your baby is unwell. If you are worried about fever, feeding, sleepiness or crying, contact your pediatrician.

Is my due date saved anywhere?

Only on this device. It is kept in your browser so the page remembers it, and it is never sent to a server. Press Clear to remove it.
